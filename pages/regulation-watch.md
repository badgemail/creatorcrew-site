# Regulation Watch

Australian rules on young people and social media are actively changing. This page tracks the ones that matter for making and sharing what you create, in plain language, for every title in *The Creator Starter Kit* series.

> **Outside Australia?** Treat this page as a reference point, not a substitute for checking your own country's current rules with a parent. Several countries are changing theirs right now.

## The under-16 social media rule

Since **10 December 2025**, platforms named by Australia's online safety regulator, eSafety, have had to take reasonable steps to stop Australians under 16 from having accounts. The named platforms are Facebook, Instagram, Kick, Reddit, Snapchat, Threads, TikTok, Twitch, X and YouTube, and several other services have told eSafety they'll apply the same minimum age.

- The obligation sits with the platforms.
- It covers holding an account. Making videos, editing them, and sharing them privately — in a family group chat, at a watch-night, through school — all still work, and that's exactly what *The Creator Starter Kit* books are built around.
- The list can change. eSafety's current list is on [esafety.gov.au](https://www.esafety.gov.au/about-us/industry-regulation/social-media-age-restrictions).

## Children's Online Privacy Code

A new code under the Privacy Act, covering online services likely to be used by young people. Public consultation closed on 5 June 2026, and it must be registered by **10 December 2026**. It's expected to require data minimisation by default and a "best interests of the child" standard. This page will be updated once the final code is registered.

## Paid work and filming

In some states, if someone under a certain age is formally engaged for paid filming, performance or modelling work — not just a family hobby project — the person engaging them may need authorisation first. In NSW the age is under 15. Chapter 10 of *The Creator Starter Kit: Content* covers this; your state's rules are worth checking with a parent before saying yes to anything paid.

## What hasn't changed

A few things hold true no matter where you live: ask before you film anyone, only use music you have the rights to, and build a private portfolio for as long as a public account isn't an option.
